How this evaluation works

A text-mode simulator plays scripted customer personas (including red-team attackers, ids starting rt_) against the agent, using the same brain, prompt and tools as the voice bot, for each scenario pack (product, company and compliance rules). Each transcript is then scored twice: deterministic rule checks flag compliance violations (disclosure, DNC, rate range, guaranteed approval, callback hours, PII, ungrounded figures, scenario-specific forbidden claims), and a separate LLM judge (a different model from the agent) scores four quality dimensions from 1 to 5 with a written reason. Per-call scores are aggregated into a summary per scenario and prompt version, priced with the config price table, and two versions can be compared A/B with confidence intervals. Voice latency comes from live voice calls, not the simulator.

Calls with ≥ 1 rule violation ÷ all calls. Lower is better.

Confidence interval of a rate (Wilson), a difference of rates (Newcombe) or a mean (bootstrap). n.s. = the interval of B − A includes 0.

User stops speaking (silence) → bot starts speaking; what the caller feels.
pipeline_ms
STT final transcript → first TTS audio; our processing only, excluding endpointing and transport.

500 ms (config voice.latency_target_ms); greeting turns are excluded from percentiles.

Median and 95th percentile, linear interpolation between closest ranks.
